# ADR-MCPRE-068 Phase 1 — THM-0042, THM-0071 and THM-0012, decomposed

The last three roots: one `high` and two `medium`. All three survived falsification. No claim
corrections. One finding, on THM-0042.

These three are the most carefully bounded statements in the estate, and the decomposition
mostly confirms work a previous review already did — which is itself the result. Each scope
below reads as a list of corrections rather than caveats, and each one names what it refuses
to claim.

> **Relationship to `remaining-roots-audit-2026-09-18.md`.** That packet audited this root
> earlier in the campaign, asking *are the leaves honest, and does the graph say what the claim
> needs*. This one asks the Phase-1 question the brief puts first — *try to falsify the root
> statement before decomposing it* — and then decomposes consequence-downward. Neither
> supersedes the other: the audit's findings (probe coverage, unit width) stand as written, and
> nothing here contradicts them.

## THM-0042 — "Retained evidence is the evidence the statement was made about"

`high`. **`depends_on = []`** — the only root besides THM-0012 and THM-0091 that rests on no
other theorem. Three supporting units.

### Falsifying it

**Attempt 1 — does `depends_on = []` understate what it rests on?** This is the same shape as
THM-0074's defect, where an antecedent quantified over something the closure did not contain.
It does not recur. THM-0042's statement is an equality over a commitment's fields, and the
scope disclaims everything an edge would otherwise be needed for, by name: registration is
THM-0041's and excluded; validity of the retained bytes is excluded; completeness of the
reconstruction is excluded; collision resistance of the commitment function is excluded, the
digest being "an opaque primitive here". An empty dependency list is correct when the claim is
about one comparison and the scope refuses the rest — and this scope does.

**Attempt 2 — is the refusal of a record carrying no submission identity really established?**
This is the statement's sharpest clause, because it refuses in BOTH directions: Ok is returned
neither for a retained record that claims a submission identity nor for one that claims none.
The consequence explains why in adversarial terms — "the archivist is exactly who would
benefit from the weaker answer being indistinguishable from the stronger one". Stated and
owned. Attempt fails.

**Attempt 3 — is the conformance corpus a happy path?** The scope answers before the question:
the corpus has controls that "keep it from being a happy path — a tampered byte and a
truncation stop corresponding, and the committed bytes are compared against a fresh generation
rather than assumed". And the `s01` interop vector is DEMOTED in place rather than regenerated,
because "that no MCP-RE code produced it is the whole value of that vector". That is the right
call and the reasoning is recorded; nothing to add.

### The finding: two supporting units own no theorem, and both are unfalsified

```
http_profile.scitt_retained_correspondence  ['mutation','test']  -            theorems=[THM-0042]
http_profile.submitted_hop_identity         ['test']             DEBT=high    theorems=[]
conformance.retained_corpus                 ['test']             DEBT=high    theorems=[]
```

THM-0042 is `supported_by` three units. Only one owns a theorem. The propositions the other
two establish are stated nowhere except as sentences inside the root itself — the statement's
entire second paragraph is about `submitted_commitment`, and a third of the scope is about the
corpus.

That is the accepted "a root and its leaves under one owner" population rather than a defect,
and it is not silently accepted: **both unowned units are `tested` with no falsifier and both
carry open N1 obligations at `high`.** So the root's two most specific propositions — the
submission-identity refusal, and that the verdict is ever reached over an artifact on disk
rather than a value a test built — are the two nothing has tried to break.

Not fixed here. Splitting the root's paragraphs into theorems would be theorem inflation for
propositions that have exactly one consumer; the honest reading is that the debt rows already
name the gap, and what this packet adds is that they sit under a root and are its most
specific content.

## THM-0071 — "Every reachable in-exchange refusal has a typed provenance that reaches the record"

`medium` direct, 5 dependencies, 6 owners.

### Falsifying it

**Attempt 1 — "every refusal" versus THM-0081's four pre-exchange replies.** The same trap as
THM-0078, and this root does not fall into it: the scope bounds the claim to IN-EXCHANGE
outcomes and names THM-0081 as what enumerates the outside set. Explicitly, and as the first
of three bounds it describes as "a correction rather than a caveat".

**Attempt 2 — is "no authority's vocabulary is recorded as another authority's verdict" a
totality claim over a union that cannot be total?** It was, once. The scope records the
resolution: ADR-MCPRE-066 decided the coordinate algebra, `PolicyError -> &'static str ->
Core reason` no longer exists as a route, and "the union that was once asked to be total is
not the object of this claim". A previous review already removed the defect this attempt
looks for.

**Attempt 3 — does it claim durability?** No, twice: the consequence says the process
disappearing takes undrained records with it, and the scope says durability is THM-0070's
boundary "and is not widened here". THM-0070 is in the closure.

### Evidence

Six owners, four falsified. Two carry pre-existing debt:

```
proxy.refusal_audit_emission  DEBT=medium   (THM-0085 — the provenance REACHES the record)
proxy.refusal_provenance      DEBT=high     (THM-0046 — which authority reached it)
```

Both are the propositions this root is *about*. THM-0071's five composing facts are listed in
its own scope, and the two whose carriers nothing attacks are numbers two and three of the
five.

`proxy.refusal_provenance` now appears as unfalsified debt under THM-0071 and THM-0078 both.

## THM-0012 — "The lifecycle record cannot claim a shutdown that did not happen"

`medium`, `depends_on = []`, one owner, falsified (`mutation` present). The only root in the
estate with no debt anywhere in its closure.

### Falsifying it

**Attempt 1 — the scope says `FailedToStart` is unreachable from `Materialized`, so a serve
that never bound is recorded as `Materialized`. Does that contradict the consequence?** The
consequence is that such a runtime "cannot be recorded as a clean drained shutdown". It holds:
reaching `Stopped` requires applying `Serving` and every event after it, and a never-bound
runtime sits at `Materialized`. The record is SILENT about the failure rather than wrong about
it, which the scope says in exactly those words. The two terminal states stay
distinguishable, which is all the consequence claims.

**Attempt 2 — `RuntimeState::admits_requests` looks like a control that could be read during
serving.** The scope has already measured this and refuses it: the value is DESCRIPTIVE, no
production path consumes it, and the recorded state is never `Serving` while a request is in
flight because the serving events are applied only after `serve_fleet` returns Ok. What
confines requests to the serving interval is resource ownership — the listener exists only
inside the `serve_fleet` call. A reader who mistook it for a control would have found it
reading `Materialized` and refusing everything.

### Evidence class, and a scope that states its own limit

The scope ends: *"Evidence is `test://` only: the uniqueness-of-path argument is a match a
reviewer reads, not a proof a prover checked."*

That sentence is what ADR-MCPRE-068 §5 asks every unit to be able to say, and it was written
before this campaign. The unit IS `tested`, it names a falsifier, and the root says plainly
what its evidence is not. Nothing to correct and nothing to reclassify.
